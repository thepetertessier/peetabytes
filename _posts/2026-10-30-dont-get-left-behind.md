### I was a bit late to the AI coding game

I remember reading Matt Shumer's [Something Big is Happening](https://somethingbig.ai/something-big-is-happening) way back in February. The point was basically that AI really is something revolutionary, it's not just hype, partially backed by his own experience of agents actually making intelligent judgment calls and autonomously developing just as a human would.

At the time, I thought that was cool, and would probably say that I agreed with him, but I didn't truly grasp it. At the time I wasn't using the frontier capabilties - I was still using VS Code Copilot with prompts here and there to build so-and-so feature or tests. Nothing truly autonomous. And I would even hand-tweak to my taste after. In reality I thought the hype-posters must have had a lower bar for quality - *There's no way this would work as driving a complex, maintainable feature from beginning to end.*

### Everything changed when I started using Claude Code with a Max subscription

My company thought it would be a cool idea if they picked young, bright developers, gave them a Claude Code Max subscription with a complex project, and saw what happened.

For the first time, I finally understood what everyone was on about.

Fable and even Opus were on another level from anything I had done before. They had taste. They understood intent. They knew what questions to ask, what commands to run, how to verify their work, self-awareness of what they could and couldn't do. For the first time reviewing the agents' work I thought, *Wow, I can't think of any way to make this better.*

I remember I asked to set up dictation on my computer so I could just press a keyboard combination and record my voice, pasting the text anywhere my cursor was. It downloaded an open source library, set up Hammerspoon on my Mac, and just got it done. I use it all the time.

I also asked it to make a UI to help me track and log my hours so I didn't have to do it manually. Boom, one-shotted it. I use it every day.

### I could go on and on about all the things the frontier AI have enabled me to do.

My productivity has skyrocketed, I'm able to make such a higher-level impact, it really is everything that people say it is.

My point is not to catalogue every way the frontier has impressed me - just that I want you to avoid making my mistake. You can read all the articles, watch all the videos of AI power-users you want, but **you will fall behind if you're not going out there and doing it yourself.**

Seriously, I've learned so so so much more just by doing and getting my hands dirty than by fawning over [Kun Chen's](https://www.youtube.com/@kunchenguid) insane hyper-optimized agent fleet or analyzing [Matt Pocock's](https://www.youtube.com/@mattpocockuk) famous agent skills.

### But Peter, won't the AI get so good that I won't have to learn how to use it?

It's conceivable - more than I'd like to admit - that even the work of *using AI* will be taken by AI and there's nothing left for us to do, so *"why bother?"*. Or that at the current breakneck speed of progress, today's AI technology will be totally obsolete in a year or so, so why not wait until it's stable - *"Then I'll learn"*.

Even if these outcomes are possible, I would still argue - **it's better to be familiar with the technology of today and be a participant than get left behind as a bystander.** Here's what I mean: the hard lessons I'm learning today were already figured out by the Matt Pococks and Kun Chens months ago. I'm still catching up, and I'd be in a better position if I was fully engaging with the technology sooner.

It's not too late to start today! Even if your company isn't giving you the frontier capabilties, try out the $20 Claude Code plan and really give it a chance. I doubt you'll be dissapointed.
