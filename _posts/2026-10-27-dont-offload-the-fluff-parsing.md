### Yesterday I was in the AI trenches.

Juggling six different Claude Code sessions, trying to steer my AI colony while trying to keep my last sliver of touch with the codebase. I then see my team lead tagged me with an ask that has a page-long description.

At this point, my brain-dead mush of a mind was at its limit, so out of laziness, I did the crime:
- copy-paste the ask into Claude
- do what it says
- paste its reply to the team lead

Yes, I became a high-latency [meat proxy](https://gruhn.me/blog/2026-08-03/).

### Fast forward to today,

she asks me for the list of open questions from me on her plate, so I included this as one of them. Embarassingly, I didn't even stop to understand what my ask was.

It turns out it wasn't an *"ask"*, it was really just an *acknowledgement* that could have been shortened to "ok, got it". But the way Claude writes, it took two or three re-reads to understand that 🙄.

### Don't offload the fluff-parsing.

**If you don't have the time or brainpower to write your own responses, you're going too fast!** Even if you have to slow down, touch grass for a moment, and return with a fresh mind and a hot espresso, your team will thank you for giving them human-generated, digestible material. That's always better than launching unintelligible globs of text at machine speed.

In this situation, I took another look at those open "asks". For any AI-generated pastes I actually went and rewrote it, distilling the content to what I actually need eyes on. Ultimately, this is better for my own understanding and the team's attention.

Until AI gets good at actually sounding human and giving us the meat, no fluff, **we're all professional fluff-parsers.** Maybe we can at least take some solace in the fact that at least for now, AI isn't better at fluff-parsing that than us.

Good luck out there in the trenches ☕.
